1. INTRODUCTION & PURPOSE OF NAGRAPH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NAGraph is a specialized, publication-grade Python-based bibliometric and network 
analysis suite designed to bridge classical CiteSpace structural depth with 
high-throughput bibliographic fetching via OpenAlex. Optimized specifically for 
optical physics, laser physics, and photothermal spectroscopy (such as laser-induced 
thermal lens and photothermal spectroscopy research), NAGraph eliminates cross-disciplinary 
noise and generates rigorous, sectionalized Word (.docx) reports.

This manual provides an exhaustive, section-by-section interpretation guide for 
every table, figure, metric, and analytical artifact contained in the generated 
NAGraph Research Report.

2. SECTION-BY-SECTION REPORT INTERPRETATION GUIDE

SECTION 1: SEARCH PARAMETERS
- What it shows: Displays a structured audit table containing the exact search keywords, 
  matching mode ("any" vs. "all"), publication year range (e.g., 2008–2026), 
  retrieval sorting criteria (relevance, cited count, or date), one-hop reference 
  expansion status, recent-work boost status, and the primary data source (OpenAlex).
- How to interpret: Ensures absolute transparency, provenance tracking, and 
  methodological reproducibility for your literature search and corpus assembly.

SECTION 2: DATA COVERAGE & NODE CAP DISCLOSURE
- What it shows: Details the exact quantitative breakdown of corpus construction: 
  how many base records were retrieved, how many recent articles were added via 
  the recent-work boost, and how many cited references were incorporated via 
  one-hop expansion. It also reports the Node Cap disclosure (e.g., total candidate 
  pool versus the enforced network ceiling of 300 nodes).
- How to interpret: Evaluates dataset comprehensiveness and explains how lower-priority 
  outliers were filtered out before network mapping.

SECTION 3: TOPIC OVERVIEW (EXTRACTIVE SUMMARY)
- What it shows: A high-level qualitative overview generated via extractive sentence ranking 
  across the corpus. It outlines total analyzed works, citation linkages, publication 
  windows, and explicitly identifies the highest-ranked flagship publication.
- How to interpret: Provides an immediate narrative summary of the core research domain's 
  breadth and highlights its most pivotal foundational study.

SECTION 4: NETWORK STATISTICS & DOCUMENT CITATION NETWORK MAP (FIGURE 1)
- What it shows: 
  * Network Statistics Summary Card: Quantifies total analyzed works (nodes), citation 
    linkages (edges), thematic clusters, unconnected works, and core global topological 
    indices: **Global Modularity ($Q$)** and **Weighted Mean Silhouette Score**.
  * Document Citation Network Map (Figure 1): A directed spring-layout graph visualizing 
    citation flow among the retrieved corpus.
- How to interpret: 
  * Modularity ($Q$) measures the strength of division of a network into distinct communities (values > 0.5 indicate clear modular structure).
  * The Weighted Mean Silhouette score evaluates cluster homogeneity and cohesion.

SECTION 5: HISTORICAL TIMELINE SLICING & CORPUS EVOLUTION
- What it shows: Groups corpus publications and cumulative citation impact across 
  customizable multi-year temporal windows (e.g., 4-year slices from 2008 to 2027).
- How to interpret: Illustrates the chronological maturation of the field, revealing 
  historical periods of rapid academic growth and surge versus phases of consolidation.

SECTION 6: STRUCTURED CLUSTER PROFILES & CHRONOLOGICAL READING PATH
- What it shows: 
  * Structured Cluster Profiles Table: Major thematic research fronts derived from network 
    clustering, detailing Cluster ID, LLR Label, Size, extracted Key Terms, Active Years, 
    and the Leading Work.
  * Chronological Reading Path: A syllabus-style table sorting foundational core papers 
    strictly from oldest to newest to guide researchers through the evolution of the field.
- How to interpret: Offers instant thematic identification and historical context for 
  every sub-specialty within optical physics.

SECTION 7: KEYWORD THEMATIC CATEGORIZATION & CITATION BURSTS
- What it shows: 
  * Thematic Keyword Tiers: Groups extracted domain terminology into functional categories 
    (Material & Synthesis, Sensing Mechanisms & Techniques, Sensing Platforms & Devices, 
    Characterization & Detection, and Environmental & Thermal Applications).
  * Keyword Citation Bursts: Identifies terms experiencing intense citation surges.
  * Reference Citation Bursts: Tracks landmark publications experiencing sudden spikes in citation activity based on Kleinberg burst detection algorithms.
- How to interpret: Maps both conceptual vocabulary tiers and temporal acceleration points 
  driving active research fronts.

SECTION 8: AUTHOR PRODUCTIVITY & TRUE AUTHOR CO-CITATION ANALYSIS (ACA)
- What it shows: 
  * Author Productivity Table: Ranks contributing authors by productivity, scores, and active spans.
  * True Author Co-Citation Analysis (ACA) Table: Maps foundational scholars whose works are co-cited across corpus reference lists, detailing co-citation counts and centrality.
- How to interpret: Separates direct author publication counts from true co-citation network structures.

SECTION 9: INSTITUTIONAL & COUNTRY COLLABORATION DISTRIBUTION
- What it shows: Geographic and organizational publication output, collaboration network 
  centralities, and temporal mean publication years for top institutions and country codes.
- How to interpret: Pinpoints global research hubs and international collaboration trends.

SECTION 10: JOURNAL CO-CITATION ANALYSIS & SOURCE DISTRIBUTION
- What it shows: Core publishing venues ranked by article volume, network centrality, 
  and representative Impact Factors.
- How to interpret: Identifies primary peer-reviewed dissemination channels shaping the domain.

SECTION 11 & APPENDIX: PAPER ROLES & NODE INTERCONNECTIVITY TRACKING
- What it shows: 
  * Paper Roles: Tags foundational, review/hub, bridge, and recent influential works.
  * Node Interconnectivity & Citation Flow Tracking Table (Node Table): Explicitly maps incoming ("Cited By Nodes") and outgoing ("Cites Nodes") citation linkages for top-ranked network nodes.
- How to interpret: Provides complete mathematical auditability and provenance traceability for network connectivity.

3. DETAILED METHODOLOGICAL GLOSSARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- PageRank: A link-analysis algorithm measuring structural importance by evaluating incoming citations.

- In-Network Citations: Citations received strictly from publications inside the assembled network.

- Global Citations: Total cumulative citation count from OpenAlex reflecting broad scholarly recognition.

- Betweenness Centrality: Quantifies how often a paper falls on the shortest path between other nodes.

- Modularity (Q): Strength of division of a network into clusters.

- Silhouette Ratio: Validation metric assessing cluster similarity and cohesion.

-How to read the Node Interconnectivity Table: A brief sentence explaining that numbers in the Cited By and  Cites columns refer to the node ranks of connected papers within the network.

-The distinction between Global and In-Network citations: Emphasizing that high global citations show general scientific impact across the world, while high in-network citations show specific foundational importance to this specific topic.
